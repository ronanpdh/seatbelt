# 0.1.0: attack it, sandbox it, hand it over

Released 2026-09-18. Build notes: [docs/BUILD.md](../docs/BUILD.md#010-attack-it-sandbox-it-hand-it-over).

## What shipped

- **Scenario pack.** 8 adversarial YAML scenarios, each mapped to the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications and, where one exists, MITRE ATLAS. They run against a `target(rec, inputs)` callable, and every finding cites the ledger event ids that prove it.
- **Docker sandbox.** One hardened container per scenario, with no network unless the scenario sets `egress: true`.
- **Evidence pack.** One zip bound by a signed `pack.json`, re-verified offline by `seatbelt verify-pack`.
- `docs/overview.md`, and OpenSSF Best Practices evidence.

## Plan vs what shipped

- The plan also had an OpenTelemetry export and NIST AI RMF and NCSC references. Neither is in the repo; scenarios carry OWASP and ATLAS ids only.
- The plan had the pack as a report. It shipped as a signed zip, and ADR 0004 leaves rendered output for later.
- The launch post and the two planned submissions are not recorded in the repo.

## Worth studying

- A closed check vocabulary instead of Python hooks: "two ways to say one thing, and the corpus stops being data" ([ADR 0003](../docs/adr/0003-scenario-pack.md)).
- Why a `Finding` cannot exist without evidence (`min_length=1` on `evidence`).
- Deterministic zips: fixed timestamps and sorted members make two builds byte-identical ([ADR 0004](../docs/adr/0004-evidence-pack.md)).
- The sandbox flags and what each one stops, and why a timeout kills the container, not only the CLI client ([ADR 0005](../docs/adr/0005-docker-sandbox.md)).
- Why a crashing target is a result (`run.ok=False`), not a runner error ([ADR 0003](../docs/adr/0003-scenario-pack.md)).
