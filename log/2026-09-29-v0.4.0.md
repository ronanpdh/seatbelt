# 0.4.0: one row per person, and erasure

Released 2026-09-29. Build notes: [docs/BUILD.md](../docs/BUILD.md#040-one-row-per-person-and-erasure).

## What shipped

- `seatbelt report --people` counts each person once across their principal ids, and `report` takes several folders.
- `seatbelt reconstruct` shows imported answers' text, and marks what is unverified or unavailable.
- `seatbelt erase` removes a person's whole ledgers inside a signed record of hashes. It is a dry run unless given `--yes`, and nothing may be writing while it runs.
- The gateway holds a lock on its ledgers folder while it runs.

## Plan vs what shipped

- Both features close items the importer design had deferred: "who a person is" (Decided 1), and deletion, which had no tooling yet (Decided 4).

## Worth studying

- Why erasure removes whole ledgers, never events. Removing events breaks the chain and the file hash, and what remains looks the same as a tampered ledger ([erasure design](../docs/plans/2026-09-29-erasure-design.md)).
- What `erase` does not decide: whether a request must be honoured. GDPR Article 17 has exceptions, and the choice is the organisation's.
- Why people are joined by explicit ids and never by e-mail. An e-mail address can change or be absent, and some providers do not verify it ([people design](../docs/plans/2026-09-29-people-and-imported-replay.md)).
- File locks as the guarantee that nothing is writing (`seatbelt.locks`), and the importer's `.erased` suppression list.
